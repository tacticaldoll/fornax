# Source notes — a changed account of a difficult meeting

Mira described a review meeting as proof that her colleagues did not respect her. Later in the
conversation she recalled that Jo had defended her work during the same meeting, while another
colleague repeatedly interrupted her. She had initially merged these different events into one
account of what everyone intended. She still felt hurt after recognizing the distinction.

The conversation explored why correcting an account need not erase the experience it describes.
Mira wanted room to name the interruption without claiming knowledge of every colleague's motives.
She also resisted replacing her first absolute story with a new story that everybody meant well.
Neither account was supported by what she had recalled. The useful change was keeping the observed
events, interpretations of intent, and unresolved feelings distinct, while leaving room for further
conversation. No general psychological law, scoring scale, causal mechanism, diagnostic criterion,
or rule for deciding whether a person felt respected was established. The notes provide an
experience to examine, not a population-level claim or an operational decision procedure.
