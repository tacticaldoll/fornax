## Pattern

Three shapes recur, and the third is this range's own.

**A green that means less than its claim** — causes 2 and 3. One rests on a borrowed matcher whose
exemption the claim does not state; the other on an expression that cannot fail. In both the
mechanism is sound and the claim about it is wider. `AGENTS.md` already demands two negative controls
for a hand-written matcher; neither cause is covered by that demand on a **borrowed** one, or on an
assertion. The pattern-level repair landed with `2a`: `AGENTS.md` now says to probe a borrowed rule's
own boundary with a matched pair and record what the probe finds where the claim is written.

**A standard established in a round, not swept into the siblings that answer to it** — causes 4, 5
and 6, all three in `check_citations` and `check_text`. `check_text.check` was split in this range
with its docstring stating the read/judge standard, and `check_citations.citations` never received
it; the paired-optional types were built here to stop one `None` carrying several answers, and
`check_text._bytes` — in the module whose split established the discipline — kept three in one.
`AGENTS.md`'s sweep clause already says to enumerate the mechanism rather than reread the diff. What
is missing is that a sweep's **scope is the standard's consumers**, not the repaired file's
neighbours.

**A rule established in a round landing in that round's own new output** — causes 7 and 8, third
consecutive round, and this instance is wider than its predecessors rather than deeper. The previous
two each produced one instance in one table. The settling commit produced four across two files
while settling the finding that names the shape. That decides the repair: the previous round's
response was an authoring rule written **in** that same commit, and it did not bind within it, which
is why cause 7 admits `7b` rather than another rule.
